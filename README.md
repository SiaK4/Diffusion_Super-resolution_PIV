## 📑 Abstract

*High-resolution particle image velocimetry (PIV) measurements are essential for studying high–Reynolds-number turbulent flows past bluff bodies, where fine-scale spatiotemporal structures play a critical role in the underlying dynamics. However, as the Reynolds number increases, the spatial and temporal resolutions required for accurate flow reconstruction escalate, placing severe demands on experimental hardware and data acquisition systems. Consequently, high-resolution PIV is often restricted to a limited field of view (FOV) in the wake region, whereas lower-resolution measurements can be captured across a much larger spatial domain. To overcome this limitation, we explore a learning-based framework that infers high-resolution velocity fields from full-field low-resolution and limited high-resolution observations available within the restricted field of view. Once trained, this mapping is applied to the low-resolution measurements over the full domain to reconstruct globally consistent, high-resolution flow
fields. Specifically, we employ conditional diffusion models to capture the stochastic distribution of high-resolution velocity fields conditioned on low-
resolution inputs. Using the low-resolution PIV data as a low-fidelity conditioning signal, the model successfully reconstructs fine-scale flow statistics while preserving physical phase coherence and spatiotemporal consistency.*
